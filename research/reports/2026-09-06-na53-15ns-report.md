## 1. Aim

Fold the **NA53 single-stranded DNA aptamer** (75 nucleotides) from its AlphaFold 3 predicted structure into a thermodynamically stable conformation under physiological conditions (310.15 K, 1.0 bar, explicit TIP3P water + 0.15 M NaCl), and characterize its:

- **Global fold stability** — RMSD relative to the starting structure, radius of gyration (Rg)
- **Internal hydrogen bonding** — number of intra-aptamer H-bonds as a proxy for folding stability
- **Flexibility profile** — per-residue root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) to identify rigid cores vs. flexible loops/tails
- **Conformational ensemble** — principal component analysis (PCA) and cluster analysis to assess whether the trajectory samples a dominant basin or remains exploratory
- **Surface accessibility** — solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) for biosensor tethering considerations

The broader goal is a **biosensing-ready structural model**: NA53 is intended as a recognition element, and its 3′ end must remain accessible for sensor attachment while the core maintains a stable fold for target binding.

## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Structure Preparation (Phase 0)

**Input:** AlphaFold 3 prediction of the NA53 ssDNA aptamer (75 nt), provided as `structures/NA53_initial.pdb`.

**Rationale for using AF3 instead of a homology model:**
- NA53 has no close structural homolog in the PDB — it is a *de novo* aptamer sequence selected for a specific target.
- AF3 (released September 2024) is the only structure predictor that handles single-stranded nucleic acids with conformational sampling competitive with specialized tools like FARFAR2 and SimRNA.
- The AF3 model provides a physically plausible starting conformation (secondary structure elements, stacking interactions) that is superior to a fully extended chain for MD initialization.

**Processing:**
1. The AF3 CIF output was converted to PDB format.
2. `validate_na53_pdb.py` checked for: complete nucleotides (all backbone atoms present), correct residue naming (DT/DA/DC/DG for DNA — *not* RNA), no missing heavy atoms, reasonable bond lengths.
3. The structure was confirmed to be DNA (not RNA) — NA53 sequence ends with `...GTAAA CCCTATGCGTGCTACCGTGAA`, all thymidines, consistent with DNA aptamer chemistry.

**Decision — why not build from sequence?**
- `gmx pdb2gmx` requires a complete 3D structure. Building from sequence alone would require an external folding tool (RNAComposer, SimRNA, etc.) which adds a dependency and an error source.
- AF3 gives a folded starting point that accelerates convergence vs. an extended chain (though 15 ns is insufficient for full convergence regardless).

**Decision — DNA force field choice: AMBER99SB-ILDN**
- AMBER99SB-ILDN is the most widely validated force field for DNA in GROMACS ecosystems.
- It includes the ILDN (improved lysine, aspartate, asparagine) corrections that improve backbone torsion sampling.
- For RNA, OL3/OL15 would be preferred, but NA53 is DNA (thymidines, not uridines).
- Alternative considered: CHARMM36, which has excellent DNA parameters but requires CHARMM-specific water models and topology tools that are less integrated with the GROMACS+pdb2gmx workflow used here.

**Decision — water model: TIP3P**
- TIP3P is the default water model paired with AMBER force fields in most GROMACS tutorials and is well-validated for nucleic acid simulations.
- TIP4P/D sensitive would be more accurate for density but adds computational cost (~15-20%) with marginal benefit for a 15 ns pilot whose purpose is method validation, not production-quality thermodynamics.
- The water model must match the force field's parameterization; AMBER99SB-ILDN + TIP3P is a standard, validated combination.

### 3.2 System Preparation (Phase 1)

**Box definition:**
- Cubic box with 1.0 nm minimum distance from any atom to the box edge (`gmx editconf -d 1.0`).
- **Rationale:** 1.0 nm is the standard cutoff-safe distance — ensures no atom interacts with its own periodic image through the 0.8 nm non-bonded cutoff. Smaller boxes risk artifacts; larger boxes waste computation on irrelevant solvent.

**Solvation:**
- `gmx solvate` with TIP3P water (pre-packaged with GROMACS).
- **Rationale:** Explicit solvation is essential for aptamer folding — the hydrophobic effect and ion screening drive collapse. Implicit solvent (GB/PB) would be faster but cannot capture the specific ion-DNA interactions critical for aptamer structure.

**Ion addition:**
- `gmx genion` to neutralize the system and achieve 0.15 M NaCl.
- NA53 is a 75-nt DNA strand with ~75 phosphate charges (−75 e). Neutralization requires 75 Na⁺, plus additional Na⁺/Cl⁻ pairs for 0.15 M concentration.
- **Rationale:** 0.15 M is physiological salt concentration. DNA aptamers are highly charged; without counterions the system would be unstable and the aptamer would not fold realistically. The Debye length at 0.15 M is ~0.8 nm, comparable to the box size — ions are necessary for realistic electrostatics.

**Force field parameters confirmed:**
| Parameter | Value | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| rcoulomb | 0.8 nm | Standard cutoff; matched across all stages |
| rvdw | 0.8 nm | Matched to rcoulomb for consistent treatment |
| Electrostatics | PME (prod) | Particle-Mesh Ewald for long-range; essential for charged DNA |
| VDW treatment | Potential-shift-Verlet | Smooth cutoff, no energy discontinuity at r_c |
| Dispersion correction | -dfdt energy (autoTypes) | Accounts for truncated VDW tail |

### 3.3 Energy Minimization (Phase 1, part 1)

**Method:** Steepest descent, max 50,000 steps, Fmax < 1000 kJ/mol/nm convergence criterion.

**Rationale:**
- The AF3 model, while physically plausible, has steric clashes from the modeling process and solvation step. EM resolves these before dynamics begin.
- Steepest descent is robust for initial bad contacts; conjugate gradient or L-BFGS would be faster but steepest descent is the standard first step and is sufficient for a system of this size.
- Fmax < 1000 kJ/mol/nm is the standard GROMACS convergence criterion — below this, forces are small enough that MD integration is stable.

**Result:** EM converged to Fmax = < 1000 kJ/mol/nm (potential energy stabilized at −4.86 × 10⁶ kJ/mol).

### 3.4 NVT Equilibration (Phase 2, part 1)

**Duration:** 100 ps  
**Thermostat:** V-rescale (modified Berendsen), tau_t = 0.1 ps, T₀ = 310.15 K  
**Position restraints:** On all heavy atoms (POSRES)  
**Time step:** 2 fs (with LINCS constraints on bonds involving hydrogen)

**Rationale:**
- V-rescale is the recommended thermostat for GROMACS — it produces a correct canonical ensemble (unlike plain Berendsen which is not a proper thermostat for production).
- 310.15 K = 37°C = physiological temperature. Aptamers function in biological conditions; 300 K (27°C) would be room temperature, not physiological.
- 100 ps is sufficient to equilibrate temperature without allowing significant structural drift (position restraints hold the structure).
- Position restraints prevent the aptamer from unfolding during the initial temperature ramp — the solvent and ions equilibrate around the fixed aptamer first.

**Result:** Temperature stabilized at 310.14 ± 0.59 K (within 0.1 K of target).

### 3.5 NPT Equilibration (Phase 2, part 2)

**Duration:** 600 ps total (100 ps NPT1 restrained + 500 ps NPT2 free)  
**Barostat:** Parrinello-Rahman, tau_p = 2.0 ps, P₀ = 1.0 bar  
**Reference coordinate scaling:** com (center-of-mass)  
**Position restraints:** ON for NPT1 (100 ps), OFF for NPT2 (500 ps)

**Rationale:**
- Parrinello-Rahman is the recommended barostat for NPT production — it produces correct fluctuations (unlike Berendsen pressure coupling which suppresses fluctuations).
- 1.0 bar = atmospheric pressure = standard state. No special pressure conditions.
- Two-stage NPT: NPT1 with restraints lets the solvent density equilibrate around the fixed aptamer; NPT2 without restraints lets the aptamer relax into the equilibrated solvent.
- 500 ps NPT2 free is sufficient for initial density stabilization and modest aptamer relaxation, but not for full equilibration of the fold — that's what production is for.
- tau_p = 2.0 ps is the standard Parrinello-Rahman coupling constant; shorter would over-couple (suppress density fluctuations), longer would under-couple (slow density equilibration).

**Result:** Density stabilized at 988.6 kg/m³ (within 1.2% of TIP3P's expected ~997 kg/m³ at 310 K; slight deviation is normal for a solvated biomolecule system where the solute displaces some water volume).

### 3.6 Production MD (Phase 3)

**Duration:** 15.000 ns (7,500,000 steps × 2 fs)  
**Ensemble:** NPT (Parrinello-Rahman, 1.0 bar)  
**Thermostat:** V-rescale, 310.15 K, tau_t = 0.1 ps  
**Position restraints:** NONE (unrestrained)  
**Electrostatics:** PME, 0.8 nm real-space cutoff  
**Output frequency:** XTC every 10 ps (1,500 frames), energy every 10 ps, checkpoint every 15 min

**Rationale:**
- 15 ns was the *actual* run length (see §1 note about the requested-vs-verified discrepancy). The original intent was 100 ns, but the run produced 15 ns due to the NS_LENGTH template bug (INCIDENT 2026-09-06).
- 2 fs timestep is standard for biomolecular MD with hydrogen constraints. Longer timesteps (4 fs with virtual sites) would be faster but require more setup and the benefit is marginal for a pilot.
- 10 ps XTC output gives 1,500 frames over 15 ns — sufficient for RMSD/RMSF analysis (10 ps sampling captures ps-ns dynamics; slower conformational changes are undersampled, which is expected at 15 ns).
- 15 min checkpoint interval is standard — frequent enough for reliable restart, not so frequent as to I/O-bottleneck the run.
- Unrestrained production: positions are free to move. This is the actual sampling phase.

**Hardware:** Taiwania 3, ct56 partition, 56 CPU cores, 32 GB allocated (node has 754 GB).

**Throughput:** 18.315 ns/day (measured from prod.log Performance line).

**Rationale — why 18.3 ns/day?**
- This is lower than typical GROMACS CPU throughput (~40-70 ns/day for similar systems) because the conda-forge build is CPU-only (no GPU acceleration on ct56 partition).
- GPU acceleration (ngs1gpu partition, Tesla GPUs) would likely achieve 50-100+ ns/day for this system size, but the GPU partition was unavailable/unconfigured at runtime.
- 18.3 ns/day is sufficient for a pilot and proves the pipeline works; GPU acceleration is the clear next step for production-length runs.

## 5. Results

### 5.1 Global Stability

#### RMSD (Cα/P backbone)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| RMSD at 15 ns (final) | ~0.79 nm (backbone) |
| 1st-half mean RMSD | ~0.70 nm |
| 2nd-half mean RMSD | ~0.79 nm |
| Trend | Still rising — not converged |

**Interpretation:** The RMSD is still increasing at 15 ns, with the second half (~0.79 nm) higher than the first half (~0.70 nm). This means the aptamer is still exploring conformational space and has not settled into a stable basin. The trajectory is **not converged** — conclusions about the "final" structure are provisional.

**Decision — RMSD reference:** RMSD was calculated relative to the *starting* AF3 structure (not a minimized or averaged structure). This is the most conservative choice — it shows the total deviation from the prediction. An RMSD-to-average would be lower and could be misleading about convergence.

#### Radius of Gyration (Rg)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Rg (total, final) | 3.548 ± 0.10 nm |
| Rg (mean over trajectory) | ~3.55 nm |
| Rg range | ~3.35–3.75 nm |

**Interpretation:** The aptamer has collapsed from an extended conformation to a partially-compact globule with Rg ≈ 3.5 nm. This is consistent with a 75-nt ssDNA forming a collapsed but not tightly packed structure — the electrostatic repulsion of the phosphate backbone prevents full collapse, while base stacking and hydrophobic interactions drive partial compaction.

**Important correction:** An earlier report cited Rg = 2.94 nm, which did not match any column of the actual `gyrate.xvg` file. The true total Rg (s0 column) is 3.548 nm. This correction was made after deep verification of the raw data against the .xvg file.

#### Internal Hydrogen Bonds

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Mean intra-DNA H-bonds | ~71 |
| Range | ~55–85 |
| Pattern | Fluctuating but sustained |

**Interpretation:** ~71 persistent internal H-bonds indicates a structured but dynamic fold. The aptamer forms a hydrogen-bonded network but is not rigid — H-bond count fluctuates by ±15, consistent with transient secondary structure elements breaking and reforming. This is expected for a 75-nt ssDNA at 310 K.

### 5.2 Flexibility Profile (RMSF)

| Region | Residues | RMSF | Interpretation |
|--------|----------|------|----------------|
| Rigid core | 22–26 | Low | Stable structured element |
| Flexible loop | 31–35 | High | Exposed, dynamic loop |
| 3′ tail | 73–75 | Very high | Free end, unstructured |

**Interpretation:**
- **Rigid core (residues 22–26):** This region has the lowest RMSF and likely forms a stable structural motif (possibly a stem or tetraloop). This is the most likely target-binding epitope if the aptamer binds via a structured pocket.
- **Flexible loop (residues 31–35):** Higher fluctuation suggests an exposed, dynamic region. This could be a binding loop that conformational-selects the target, or simply an unstructured region.
- **3′ tail (residues 73–75):** Very high RMSF — the free 3′ end is completely unstructured. This is *good* for biosensor applications: the 3′ end is accessible for sensor tethering without disrupting the folded core.

### 5.3 Conformational Ensemble (PCA + Clustering)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| PCA modes computed | From covar.mat (all Cα atoms) |
| PC1 variance | ~35.1% |
| Number of clusters (0.2 nm RMSD cutoff) | 347 |
| Largest cluster occupancy | ~0.7% (2–3 frames) |

**Interpretation:**
- **347 clusters at 0.2 nm cutoff** with no dominant cluster means the trajectory samples a broad conformational space with no single basin dominating. This is the statistical signature of an **unconverged** simulation — a converged 15 ns trajectory of a small aptamer would typically show 1–3 dominant clusters.
- **PC1 variance of 35.1%** is moderate — the first principal component captures a significant but not overwhelming fraction of the total motion, consistent with broad exploration rather than oscillation around a single basin.
- **Conclusion:** The 15 ns trajectory does not support a claim of "the NA53 structure is X." It supports a claim that "NA53 forms a partially-collapsed globule with a rigid core (22–26), a flexible loop (31–35), and an accessible 3′ tail, but the conformational ensemble is not yet converged."

**Decision — cluster cutoff 0.2 nm:** This is a standard RMSD cutoff for clustering biomolecular trajectories. Tighter cutoffs (0.1 nm) would give more clusters; looser (0.5 nm) would merge distinct conformations. 0.2 nm is a reasonable middle ground that reveals the lack of convergence.

### 5.4 Solvent Accessibility (SASA)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Mean SASA | ~Stable throughout trajectory |
| Pattern | Slightly decreasing trend |

**Interpretation:** SASA is stable with a slight decreasing trend, consistent with the ongoing collapse (Rg decreasing slightly). The aptamer surface is accessible to solvent, which is relevant for biosensor tethering — chemical modifications can be attached to exposed positions.

## 6. Inferences

### 6.1 Structural Biology Inferences

1. **NA53 forms a partially-collapsed globule, not an extended chain.** The Rg of 3.5 nm for a 75-nt strand (contour length ~25 nm if fully extended) indicates significant compaction — roughly 7-fold reduction in end-to-end distance. This is consistent with ssDNA adopting a dynamic, partially-folded conformation in solution.

2. **There is a structured core (residues 22–26) and flexible periphery.** The RMSF profile identifies a rigid region that may be the functional epitope and flexible regions (loop 31–35, tail 73–75) that provide conformational adaptability.

3. **The 3′ end is accessible for biosensor tethering.** The 3′ tail (residues 73–75) is completely unstructured and highly solvent-exposed. This is the ideal attachment point for a biosensor — the tether can be placed here without disrupting the folded core.

4. **The trajectory is NOT converged.** The RMSD is still rising, and the cluster analysis shows 347 clusters with no dominant basin. Any statement about "the NA53 structure" must be qualified as provisional, based on a non-converged 15 ns sample.

### 6.2 Biosensing Implications

1. **The 3′ tail is the recommended tethering site.** It is unstructured, solvent-exposed, and distant from the rigid core — modification here is least likely to disrupt folding or target binding.

2. **The 31–35 loop is a candidate target-binding region.** Its flexibility suggests it could undergo induced fit upon target binding. If the aptamer's target is known, this loop's conformational dynamics are relevant to binding mechanism.

3. **A stable core (22–26) provides a folding anchor.** If the aptamer is to be used in a biosensor where consistent folding is critical (e.g., for reproducible target binding), the rigid core suggests the fold is robust to moderate perturbations.

### 6.3 Methodological Inferences

1. **AF3 provides a usable starting structure.** The AF3 model was complete, correctly typed as DNA, and produced a stable simulation without topological issues. The pdb2gmx step succeeded without the problems that plague incomplete or mis-typed inputs.

2. **The pipeline works end-to-end.** All stages (predict → prep → equil → prod → analysis) executed successfully on the cluster, producing a complete trajectory and full analysis suite. The infrastructure is validated.

3. **CPU throughput (18.3 ns/day) is limiting.** For a 100 ns production run at this rate, ~5.5 days would be needed, exceeding the ct56 4-day limit. GPU acceleration or a longer queue time (with RESTART) is required for production-length sampling.

4. **The NS_LENGTH bug must never recur.** The 2026-09-06 incident (100 ns requested, 15 ns produced) is now permanently prevented by: (a) the `_pin_ns_length` function that unconditionally rewrites the template's default, (b) the verified-ns display in health_report and dashboard, (c) the run registry that records both requested and verified ns.
